# KOHRA design notes

The modelling choices behind the simulation, in one place. They're useful for review and for jury questions. Everything here is illustrative and unclassified.

## Terrain and data

- **Source data.** Copernicus GLO-30 tile `N11_00_E076_00`, plus an OpenStreetMap extract from a Protomaps daily build, clipped to the box (zoom 0–15) with go-pmtiles. Both are fetched once, and nothing is fetched at run time.
- **Synthetic fallback.** `scripts/synth_terrain.py` writes the same files from seeded noise, with the scenario's river, ford, ridge, hill and road built in. CI always uses it. The client shows a red SYNTHETIC TERRAIN banner while it is active. Its basemap is raster PNG tiles, and `style.ts` picks vector or raster layers from the PMTiles header.
- **Surface model.** GLO-30 is a DSM: tree canopy and buildings count as "ground" for line of sight and diffraction.
- **Elevation cross-check.**
  - Against bilinear sampling of the source GeoTIFF, all five scenario places agree within 0.35 m.
  - Over 3,000 random points, the mean difference is −0.03 m and the mean absolute difference is 0.45 m.
  - Shifting the grid by ±15 m makes both figures worse, so registration is correct.
  - Single nearest-pixel values differ by up to 1.9 m on slopes and built-up cells, because the UTM 30 m grid and the 1″ source grid are different grids.
  - The per-place table is in `data/terrain/elevation_check.json` after a build.
- **Water and movement.**
  - Only OSM water *polygons* (major rivers) are impassable, except within 150 m of a scenario ford.
  - Stream *lines* are fordable, at ×2 cost on foot and ×3 by vehicle.
  - OSM bridges are not crossings: units cross only at fords named in the scenario.
  - On the 90 m movement grid, a cell is water if at least 3 of its nine 30 m sub-cells are water, which keeps rivers continuous.
  - The 1 km margin outside the box is impassable.
- **Land cover codes:** open, water, road, built, forest, stream. The 90 m slope is the mean of the 30 m slopes in the block.

## Scenario geometry (vignette RIDGE)

The real north-east of the box is flat lowland (20–60 m), so the vignette sits on the river loop in the south-west, below the Ghats spurs. `scripts/check_scenario.py` checks each scenario intent on both real and synthetic terrain:

- **Ford OSPREY** (5 m) is the only crossing between the red bank and the blue bank. A search confirms there is no path without it.
- **KESTREL Ridge** (297 m) blocks line of sight from the ford to the red assembly area at **JUNIPER**.
- **Pt HERON** (205 m), 2.1 km south of the ford, has line of sight to it and serves as an observation post.
- **Route BLUEJAY** was generated by A* over real roads, then thinned. It crosses the river only at OSPREY.
- **Red recce** starts 2.5 km by road from the ford, so first contact lands near decision point 1 (06:04).
- **Demo jammer.**
  - First position (in among the company): all three company-net links go dark, with delivery probability near 0.
  - Second position (behind KESTREL): every company link recovers to at least 27 dB SINR.

## Engine

- **Signature.** `step(state, commands, rng, world)`. `world` is the static scenario and terrain. Its only mutable part is the link cache, which is keyed on positions snapped to 30 m cells, so a cache hit and a miss give identical values.
- **Command timing.** `state.tick` is the next tick to execute. Commands are stamped with that tick and applied in the order `(apply_tick, source_rank, source_id, client_seq)`.
- **State hashes.** A hash is taken after every 60 completed ticks and at the end.
- **Speeds.** Scenario speeds are the tactical pace. `fast` is ×1.5 on foot and ×1.25 for vehicles.
- **Route moves** follow the route's segments, joining at the segment nearest the unit and leaving at the one nearest the goal. Free moves use A*.
- **Detection.**
  - For each (observer, target) pair in range with line of sight, the best sensor makes one draw.
  - Line of sight ignores earth bulge, which is negligible at 4 km or less; diffraction includes it.
- **Attrition.**
  - Each tick, a target loses `rate[attacker type][target posture] × (tick / 60 s) × attacker strength`.
  - Every attacker in range with line of sight counts, and all losses apply at the same time.
  - Units below 30 % strength do not fire.
  - A fire mission damages every unit within its radius of a scattered impact point.
- **Orders.**
  - HOLD sets posture to dug in. OBSERVE halts the unit and records the grid.
  - ANVIL answers a FIRE_MISSION with WILCO, then "rounds complete" at impact. Any other order to ANVIL gets "cannot comply".
- **Planted reports** must name a real station as the sender. They are transmitted from that station's position, so they face the same link physics as genuine traffic.
- **GNSS walk-off.** Inside a spoofing zone, a unit's believed position drifts at a set rate and bearing, up to a cap. After leaving, the offset decays at the zone's recovery rate.

## Comms model

- **Path loss** = max(free-space, plane-earth) + single knife-edge diffraction + a fixed extra loss.
  - Plane-earth gives the 40 dB/decade fall-off of ground-level VHF.
  - Diffraction applies only when terrain cuts the line of sight. Partial Fresnel-zone clearance is already inside the plane-earth term.
- **SINR** is computed at each receiver, against every active jammer weighted by its frequency overlap with the net.
- **Delivery probability** = logistic(a × (worst SINR during the message's airtime − θ)), with a = 0.7 per dB and θ = 6 dB.
- **Intermittent jammers** switch on and off as a two-state Gilbert–Elliott chain.
- **Delay** comes from a per-net precedence queue (FLASH, IMMEDIATE, PRIORITY, ROUTINE). Each net is half-duplex.

## Reports and grading

- **Grades.** Every message carries a grade, reliability A–F by credibility 1–6, set in `config/grading.yaml`. Status traffic (LOCSTAT, SITREP, WILCO, SAY AGAIN, fire status) is graded `B2`.
- **Contact numbers** (`C-1-03`) are platoon-local track numbers sent as message metadata. They are never truth IDs.
- **Vocabulary.** Order templates are `order_move`, `order_halt`, and so on. The task vocabulary is SECURE, OCCUPY, SCREEN, DEFEND and CLEAR. Place names are fictional codenames only.
- **Free-text messages** do not need a confidence. Orders always do.

## Truth isolation

`views.view_for()` is the only path from simulation state to a player socket. Truth IDs, SINR, jammer positions and planted-report flags never leave the server on `/ws/play`.

`tests/test_no_truth_leak.py` plants a hidden marker on every truth unit and checks the player's socket for them. A red unit standing exactly on a public map point (a place or a route vertex, which the player already has) does not count as a leak.

## Not yet done

- No cross-check against the NTIA ITM propagation model.
- Replay is identical on the same build and platform, not across platforms.
- The Windows CI job runs on manual trigger only, to save private-repo minutes. Development and manual testing are on Windows 11.
