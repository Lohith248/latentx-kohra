# Deviations from the M1 brief

Each item says what changed and why. Anything not listed follows the brief's defaults (section 3).

## Terrain and data

1. **Terrain is REAL.** Copernicus GLO-30 `N11_00_E076_00` plus the Protomaps daily build of 2026-10-04,
   extracted for the bbox at z0–15 with go-pmtiles 1.31.2. Synthetic terrain (`scripts/synth_terrain.py`)
   is used only in CI and as the fallback. The client shows a SYNTHETIC TERRAIN banner when it is in use.
2. **Elevation check (K-01).** The 5 points are the scenario places, snapped to the nearest 30 m cell
   centre of `terrain.npz`, so the check compares the reprojected value itself rather than a second
   interpolation of it. Against bilinear sampling of the source GeoTIFF at the same lon/lat, all 5 agree
   within 0.35 m. Against the nearest source pixel (what `gdallocationinfo` returns by default), only 1 of
   5 agrees within 1 m (OSPREY, 0.45 m); the others are off by 1.07 to 1.9 m (worst: TAMARIND, a built-up
   DSM cell). This is because the UTM 30 m grid and the 1-arcsecond source grid are different grids. Over 3,000 random points the mean
   difference is −0.03 m and the mean absolute difference is 0.45 m. Shifting the grid ±15 m makes both
   worse, which shows there is no registration error. Numbers are in the README.
3. **What blocks movement.** Only OSM water *polygons* (major rivers) are impassable, except within
   150 m of a scenario ford. River and stream *lines* are fordable at a cost of ×2 on foot and ×3 by
   vehicle. Otherwise every small tributary in this box would wall units in. Water beats road, so OSM
   bridges are not crossings (D23: crossings exist only at scenario fords). On the 90 m grid a cell
   counts as water if at least 3 of its nine 30 m sub-cells are water, so rivers stay continuous. The
   1 km margin outside the bbox has no OSM data and is impassable.
4. **Synthetic basemap is a raster** (PNG tiles in `basemap.pmtiles`), so no MVT encoder is needed.
   `style.ts` picks vector or raster layers from the PMTiles header tile type, which the server reports
   in `hello`.
5. **Land cover codes** add `stream` (5) to open, water, road, built and forest. The 90 m slope is the
   mean of the 30 m slopes in each block.

## Engine

6. **`step(state, commands, rng, world)`** takes a fourth argument: `world` is static scenario and terrain
   context. It is never mutated except for the link cache, whose values are computed from positions
   snapped to 30 m cells, so a cache hit and a miss give identical results.
7. **Command timing (D5).** `state.tick` is the next tick to execute (last completed + 1). Commands
   received now get `apply_tick = state.tick`, which is the brief's `current_tick + 1`.
8. **Hash points.** A hash is taken after 60, 120, … completed ticks, and at the end (the end coincides
   with 720 in the vignette).
9. **Vehicle speeds.** The scenario speeds are the tactical pace. `fast` is ×1.25 for vehicles, because
   the schema defines `foot_fast` but no vehicle fast speed.
10. **Route moves** follow straight segments between route vertices, joining at the segment nearest the
    unit and leaving at the segment nearest the goal. They do not use A*.
11. **Detection.** For each (observer, target) pair the sensor with the highest `p` is used, with one
    `detect` draw per pair in range and LOS. LOS ignores earth bulge, which is negligible at ≤ 4 km.
    Diffraction does include the bulge.
12. **Attrition.** The target loses `rate[attacker_type][target_posture] × 100 % × (tick/60 s) ×
    attacker_strength/100` for each attacker in range with LOS (2 m to 2 m), and all losses apply
    simultaneously. Units below `combat_ineffective_below` do not fire. Fire missions damage every unit,
    on either side, within `radius_m` of the scattered impact (CEP to σ: ÷1.1774).
13. **Orders.** HOLD gives posture `dug_in`. OBSERVE halts the unit and records the grid, with no sensor
    bonus in M1. ANVIL answers FIRE_MISSION with WILCO, then sends "rounds complete" (fire_status) at
    impact. Any other order to ANVIL gets "cannot comply".
14. **Planted reports** must name a real station as `from_callsign`. They are transmitted from that
    unit's position, so they pass through the same link physics.
15. **GNSS recovery.** A unit remembers the `recover_mps` of the zone it was last in and decays at that
    rate after leaving it (default 1 m/s).

## Reports and grading

16. **Status traffic grade.** LOCSTAT, SITREP, WILCO, SAY AGAIN, fire status and unscripted Bn HQ
    traffic carry `status_grade: B2`, a new key in `config/grading.yaml`, so every report has a grade.
17. **Contact numbers** (`C-1-03`) travel as message metadata (`contact_no`) because the fixed
    templates have no slot for them. They are platoon-local track numbers, not truth IDs.
18. **Template keys.** Order templates are `order_move`, `order_halt`, and so on, to keep them distinct
    from report types. The extra vocabularies are `task` (SECURE, OCCUPY, SCREEN, DEFEND, CLEAR) and
    `place_name`: capitalised words, so fictional names only.
19. **Free text** (TEXT) does not need confidence or relied_on. D14 applies them to orders only, and
    the bot's TEXT line has neither.

## Tests and tooling

20. **Truth-leak test.** Red positions that coincide exactly with public scenario geometry (place
    points and route vertices, which the player already has in `hello`) are not counted as leaks. A unit
    arriving at OSPREY stands exactly on a public point.
21. **Client versions.** maplibre-gl 5.24.0, vite 6.4.3 and vitest 3.2.7 were pinned instead of the
    latest releases (maplibre 6.12, vite 8.3, vitest 5.0), which were under 7 days old
    on 2026-10-05. milsymbol is pinned at 2.2.0 instead of 3.0.4 for a known API.
22. **CI.** The Windows job runs on `workflow_dispatch` only, to save private-repo minutes.
    Development was done on Windows 11 with Node 24. CI uses Node 20 LTS.
23. **Not done (optional in the brief).** The NTIA ITM cross-check (`itmlogic`) was not run.
24. **Git identity.** Following the owner's override instruction, commits are authored as Lohith248
    (noreply address), and a commit-msg hook strips tool attribution lines.
